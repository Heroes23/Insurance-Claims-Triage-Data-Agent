### `assert_last_claim_within_inception` (warn, 1,483 rows)
**Root cause category: BAD_UPSTREAM_DATA**

**Evidence**
- **Row counts:** the test returns 1,483 of 9,134 rows (16.2%). There are 9,134 distinct customers, so each customer appears once and the 1,483 failures are 1,483 different customers.
- **Parsing is not the cause:**
  - In `RAW.AUTO_INSURANCE_BATCHES`, 0 of 9,134 rows fail `try_to_number` for either column.
  - No values contain decimals, and there are 0 nulls in either column in staging.
  - `stg_auto_insurance` only trims and casts these two fields.
- **Not tied to a batch:** every batch from 2026-01-01 to 2026-02-19 has 19–34 failing rows out of about 155. No batch is an outlier, so this is not a one-off bad load.
- **Multiple policies don't explain it:** a customer's current policy could be newer than an earlier claim. But customers with `number_of_policies = 1` fail at 16.0% (521 of 3,251), the same as the overall rate. Rates for 2–9 policies range from 13.5% to 19.6%, with no trend.
- **The two fields look independent:** the correlation between them is about -0.04. Real data would show a claim recency bounded by policy age. These look like separately generated values.

**Not established:** I can't tell from the warehouse whether the source system produces these values wrongly or the extract or generator does. The data is also not clearly wrong in the "months since last claim" sense. The field might mean something different from what the test assumes. The test's assumption needs confirming with the data owner.
